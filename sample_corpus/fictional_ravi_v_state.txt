Ravi Kumar v State of Northvale (fictional case, not real law)
1. The petitioner, a convict in Northvale Central Prison, complained that he was kept in solitary confinement for ninety days without any written order.
2. The State argued that a prisoner has no right to approach a court about prison conditions once convicted. The Court rejected that argument.
3. The Court held that a convicted prisoner keeps all fundamental rights that are not taken away by the sentence, and may petition the High Court under its writ jurisdiction against cruel or arbitrary treatment inside prison.
4. The Court directed that solitary confinement may be imposed only by a written, reasoned order that the prisoner can challenge before a magistrate.
5. The petition was allowed and the State was directed to pay compensation of rupees fifty thousand.
